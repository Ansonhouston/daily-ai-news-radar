## WHY ADOPTION IS THE PART THAT ACTUALLY FAILS

Most enterprise AI programmes do not fail on model selection or tooling. They fail because the tool lands and the workflow does not change — the pilot succeeds, the rollout stalls, and six months later usage is concentrated in the three people who were enthusiastic anyway.

I have hit that wall on a small scale and worked out what fixes it:

**Documented workflows per deliverable type, not per tool.** People do not adopt "AI". They adopt "how I now produce this specific report". The unit of adoption is the deliverable.

**A quality gate on every workflow.** The fastest way to kill enterprise AI adoption is one embarrassing output reaching a client. Every workflow I build has a named check before anything leaves the building — which is also what makes it defensible in a regulated environment.

**A transferability test.** My standard: someone in their second week should produce output at the same standard without asking anyone. Undocumented AI skill inside an organisation is a single point of failure wearing the costume of an advantage.

**Honest capacity arithmetic.** I can say what a workflow saved — 50% off testing cycles, ~60% off production time — because I measured it before and after. Vague productivity claims are why finance functions stop believing AI business cases.
