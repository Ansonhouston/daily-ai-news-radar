## THE COMMERCIAL SITUATION I'D BE HIRED INTO

Hong Kong's education sector is in a **time-boxed budget event**. Under the AI for Empowering Learning and Teaching programme, schools can receive a **one-off HK$500,000 grant** for school-based AI initiatives, against a **HK$500M** three-year envelope — sitting alongside roughly **HK$2B** of Quality Education Fund money earmarked for digital education, plus a new blueprint mandating AI literacy and teacher training.

Every school in the territory now has budget and a mandate. What they do not have is certainty about which vendor to spend it on.

That is a demand-capture problem, and it has a shape I recognise:

- **A defined buyer set** — roughly 1,000 primary and 500 secondary schools. That is a finite, addressable, nameable market. Every one of them is reachable.
- **A defined decision unit** — principal, IT coordinator, panel head, and increasingly a procurement committee. Multiple readers, different objections, one signature.
- **A defined window** — grants get allocated, then they are gone. Share of voice during the allocation period compounds; share of voice after it does not.
- **A defined competitive test** — a school comparing two vendors will ask which one is on the approved funding pathway. Any gap there is a marketing problem before it is a product problem, and it needs an answer prepared rather than improvised.

I would treat this as a campaign with a deadline, not an always-on brand programme.

## HOW I'D RUN IT

**Instrument first.** No spend goes out before GA4, UTM discipline, and a pipeline view that ties a piece of content to a demo request to a signed school. I have built exactly this at Baby Central — GA4 and Meta Ads Manager dashboards feeding monthly senior-stakeholder reporting — and I will not defend a budget I cannot attribute.

**Fix the proof layer before amplifying it.** Adoption claims currently appear as 80+, 100+, 150+ and 200+ schools across different company properties, all live at once. In a consumer funnel that is untidy; in an institutional sale it is a procurement objection waiting to happen. One verified number, applied everywhere, before a dollar goes into paid.

**Build for a committee, not a click.** A school does not convert on a landing page. The asset set is: a comparison page that survives being read next to a competitor's, a funding-application-ready one-pager a coordinator can forward upward, case evidence from named schools, and a nurture sequence that keeps the account warm across a multi-month cycle. I have run 30,000-subscriber lifecycle programmes in Klaviyo — a long consideration cycle is a lifecycle problem, and lifecycle is a discipline I already have.

**Capture the search that the funding creates.** When a principal starts spending, they search. Being the answer at that moment is worth more than any awareness campaign. I grew organic traffic 30% at Baby Central through integrated SEO and paid social, and I run AI-assisted competitor and trend monitoring that feeds directly into the next content cycle.

**Let practitioners carry it.** I built and scaled a 200+ KOL and influencer programme across HK and SEA in a trust-gated category. Teacher advocacy is the same machine: find the teachers already succeeding with the product, equip them, and let peer proof do the work that advertising cannot.

## METRIC DISCIPLINE

I read performance down the funnel, not at the top of it: **thumb-stop and hook rate → CTR → CVR → CPA → ROAS / pipeline value.** Creative that wins on engagement and dies downstream gets retired, not scaled. Budget runs on a **70/30 split** between proven and actively tested, with single-variable sequencing — hook, then message, then format — so every result is attributable and every winner is documented into a playbook rather than re-derived next quarter.

For an institutional sale I would extend that ladder to: **content engagement → qualified school enquiry → demo/pilot → signed licence → renewal**, and hold the budget accountable at the pipeline end of it.
