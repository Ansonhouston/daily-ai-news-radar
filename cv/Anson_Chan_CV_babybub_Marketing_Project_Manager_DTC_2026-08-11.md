## FIRST 90 DAYS — AGAINST YOUR OWN SHIP LIST

You said the first 90 days ship a **launch playbook, CRO QA checklist, creator payment SOP, vendor report cadence, and creative brief workflow.** Here is how I would sequence those, and why in that order.

**Weeks 1–2 — map the current state before writing any SOP.** Every live vendor: what they own, what they owe, when, and where the last three handoffs slipped. An SOP written before you know the actual failure points is documentation theatre. I would also want one number per vendor — what we pay and what it produced — because that reframes every later prioritisation argument.

**Weeks 2–4 — creative brief workflow and creator payment SOP first.** These are the two that bleed daily and cost the least to fix. A brief template with mandatory fields (objective, audience, claim boundaries, deliverable spec, review states, deadline) and a payment process with a single intake point and a defined turnaround. Creators forgive a small fee; they do not forgive being chased for their own money.

**Weeks 4–8 — launch playbook, built from a real launch, not in the abstract.** I would rather run the next launch with a rough checklist and write the playbook from what actually broke than produce a beautiful document that describes an imaginary launch. Kickoff gate, asset deadlines working backwards from live date, dependency map, go/no-go criteria, and a named owner per lane.

**Weeks 6–10 — CRO QA checklist, agreed *with* the specialist.** Written jointly, or it will not be honoured: hypothesis, variant spec, sample-size floor, kill criteria, tracking verification before launch, and a shared definition of "shipped". The checklist's job is to stop us learning nothing from a test that ran with broken tracking.

**Weeks 8–12 — vendor report cadence, one page per vendor.** Same shape for everyone, same day each month, populated from source rather than from the vendor's own narrative. Not for the vendors — for the co-founder, so a spend decision takes ten minutes instead of a week of chasing.

**Throughout — bring options, not questions.** Two or three, with a recommendation and what it costs. Founder-led chaos gets worse when the operator's contribution is more decisions to make.
