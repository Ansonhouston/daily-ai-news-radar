## HOW I'D RUN THE IN-HOUSING — FIRST 60 DAYS

**Week one is custody, not strategy.** Before anything is optimised, everything needs to be owned: Meta Business Manager admin under Mylo's own account, the pixel and Conversions API under Mylo's asset list, the product catalogue, GA4 and Google Search Console property ownership, the Shopify admin, domain and DNS access, and every ad account and page role documented. An in-housing that skips this step usually discovers the gap months later, at the worst possible moment.

**Then extract the learnings before they walk out the door.** Whatever has already been tested has value, and it evaporates when a relationship ends. I would want the account's full history pulled and written down — which audiences, creatives, offers and landing pages have been run, what won, what failed, and what was never tried. It is the cheapest research available and almost nobody does it during a handover.

**Do not rebuild the account in week one.** Conversion history and learning-phase data are real assets. The usual mistake in an in-housing is a clean-slate restructure that resets everything and produces a performance dip the founder then attributes to the new hire. I would run what exists, read it properly for two to three weeks, and restructure deliberately with a documented reason for each change.

**Establish the reporting rhythm before the first optimisation.** You asked for clear weekly and monthly reporting that summarises results, extracts learnings, flags risks and recommends next steps. I would rather set that format up in week one so the baseline is agreed and every subsequent change can be attributed — I have built exactly this at Baby Central, where GA4 and Meta dashboards fed monthly senior-stakeholder reporting.

**Then start testing, one variable at a time.** Details below.

## THE TESTING PROGRAMME

You asked for a rigorous, structured programme across audience segments, creative concepts, messaging angles, offers and landing pages — and, in your success criteria, for **documented** learnings. The documentation is the part that compounds; the tests are just the input.

**How I structure it.** A 70/30 split between proven spend and active tests. Single-variable sequencing — hook, then messaging angle, then format, then offer, then landing page — so every result is attributable to one change rather than to three at once. Winners get written into a playbook with the conditions attached, so next quarter starts from what is known instead of re-deriving it.

**Reading results down the funnel, not at the top.** Thumb-stop and hook rate → CTR → CVR → CPA → ROAS, and for a subscription-shaped business, repeat rate. Creative that wins attention and dies at checkout gets retired, not scaled — and the diagnosis lives at whichever step actually broke, which is the "data-driven diagnostics" you describe.

**What this produced.** **+200% conversion rate** for one client through exactly this discipline, running 10+ variants per cycle with AI-assisted production so testing volume did not require a bigger team. Peak campaigns at 10x+ ROAS; cycle time cut roughly 50%.

**Kill criteria, agreed in advance.** The main reason testing programmes stall is that nobody decided beforehand what "failed" looks like, so losing variants get defended. I set the threshold and the spend cap before the test runs.
