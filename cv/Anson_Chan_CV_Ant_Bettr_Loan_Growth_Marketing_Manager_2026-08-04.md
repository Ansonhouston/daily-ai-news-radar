**What transfers directly.** A lending lifecycle and an e-commerce lifecycle are the same machine with different nouns. New customer credit onboarding is activation. First disbursement is first purchase. Repeat borrowing is repurchase rate. Dormant reactivation is win-back. I have built and run every one of those stages against a P&L, including the unglamorous parts — segmentation that holds up, offer economics that do not quietly destroy margin, and reporting that survives being questioned.

**What I bring that is harder to hire.** Native writing-grade Traditional Chinese *and* English, so compliant copy does not have to route through a translator. Eight years of paid-media budget discipline, so a voucher or rate-discount campaign gets judged on cost per incremental funded customer rather than redemption volume. And an AI-assisted production system, in commercial use since 2022, that lets one person produce and test campaign variants at a volume that normally needs a team.

**What I would be learning, stated plainly.** I have not worked in consumer lending. Cash Loan and BNPL product mechanics, credit approval and drawdown funnels, and risk-control collaboration on customer tiering are all new to me, and I would expect to be useful on product economics within a quarter rather than on day one. I would rather say this now than have it surface in month two.

**What I have already done about the regulatory half.** I have read the current position rather than assumed it. Licensed money lenders sit under the **Money Lenders Ordinance** and the Registrar of Money Lenders, not the HKMA — so **Licensing Condition 9** is the operative constraint on every piece of marketing: name and licence number displayed, the prescribed risk warning in both written and audio form, nothing that exaggerates ease of approval or encourages borrowing without regard to circumstances. I also note that the **first-phase enhanced measures took effect on 1 August 2026** — the new debt-servicing-ratio caps on unsecured lending to lower-income borrowers, the prohibition on requesting loan referees, and the shift of the risk-warning wording to whatever the Companies Registry specifies. Those are not footnotes for a growth plan; the DSR caps narrow the addressable audience and the referee prohibition removes a common acquisition shortcut, which changes where acquisition spend should sit. Compliance in this category is a brief, not an obstacle — but it has to be read, not assumed, and I would rather arrive having read it.

## HOW I'D APPROACH THE FIRST 90 DAYS

**Instrument the loop before spending against it.** Before new campaign spend, I want the funnel visible end to end — traffic entry, credit application, approval, first drawdown, repeat borrowing — with a single agreed definition per stage and a dashboard that a product lead, a risk lead and a finance lead all read the same way. I have built this reporting layer before; disagreement about definitions is what quietly kills growth programmes.

**Segment on behaviour, not demographics.** Borrowing pattern, repayment performance and consumption scenario are far stronger signals than age bracket. The first analysis I would want is which segments repay well *and* borrow repeatedly, because that intersection is where acquisition spend and rate discounts are worth concentrating — and where they are not.

**Judge offers on cost per incremental funded customer.** A voucher or rate discount has to be measured against the customer it actually created, not the redemption count. That means holdout groups where feasible, and an honest read on whether a discount simply pulled forward someone who would have converted anyway. It also means pressure-testing the wording: a "0% instalment" or "interest-free" claim is only available where the borrower genuinely pays nothing above principal, so the offer design and the copy have to be settled together rather than in sequence.

**Separate the two product stories.** Cash Loan sells speed and flexibility under pressure; BNPL sells permission to spend without a penalty. Those are different emotional states and should not share creative, even when they share a customer. I would define distinct messaging pillars and test them independently.

**Build the approval workflow before the campaign calendar.** Every piece of copy has to clear Licensing Condition 9, and with the Companies Registry now able to revise the mandated risk-warning wording, that is a live dependency rather than a one-off template. What I would want in week one is a named reviewer, an agreed turnaround time, and a pre-approved claims library — so testing velocity is limited by ideas rather than by sign-off.

**If the consumer book is pre-launch, sequence accordingly.** A launch and a scale-up need different first moves: a launch needs the funnel instrumented, the claims library cleared, one segment chosen deliberately, and a small honest cohort read before spend is widened. I would rather establish which of those two situations this is in the first conversation than assume.
