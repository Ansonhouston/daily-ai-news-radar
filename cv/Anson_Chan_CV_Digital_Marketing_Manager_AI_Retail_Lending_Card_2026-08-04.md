**And I have built the governance half, which is what makes a rollout survive contact with a regulated business.** Reusable prompt templates with conditional logic, enforced QC checklists, naming and version control, and a documented approach to what may and may not be produced by a model and what always needs a human on it. ## HOW I'D RUN THE AI MANDATE — FIRST 90 DAYS

**Audit the work before proposing the tools.** The first deliverable is not a tool recommendation. It is a map of where the marketing team's hours actually go — which tasks are repetitive, which are blocked waiting on someone else, and which produce output nobody reads. AI applied to a task that shouldn't exist just produces waste faster.

**Pick two or three wins that are immediate and unarguable.** Adoption is won on the first attempt, not the tenth. I would start where the current process is visibly painful and the quality bar is objective — first-draft copy variants, competitor and market monitoring, brief and deck drafting, report summarisation — and deliberately avoid anything where a bad output is embarrassing before the workflow is proven.

**Ship templates, not encouragement.** A blank prompt box is the single biggest reason rollouts stall. What actually moves a team is a library of task-specific templates with the decision logic written in, so a colleague's first attempt succeeds without them having to learn prompt craft first.

**Build the governance gate at the same time as the capability.** In a regulated lending and card business, what a model may draft, what always requires human review, what may never be model-generated, and where the audit trail lives all have to be settled before volume — not after an incident. I would want a named reviewer, an agreed turnaround, and a pre-approved claims library so testing velocity is limited by ideas rather than by sign-off.

**Measure the rollout like a campaign.** Hours returned, cycle time, variants tested per period, and actual usage rates by team member. If adoption is not measured it silently reverts, and "we rolled out AI" becomes a statement nobody can defend six months later.

**Then compound it outward.** The mandate covers the wider company, not just marketing. Once marketing has a working pattern and a governance model that has survived scrutiny, that becomes the reference implementation other teams copy — which is a far cheaper route to company-wide adoption than starting fresh in each function.
