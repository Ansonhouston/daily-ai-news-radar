## HOW I'D RUN CLIENT OPERATIONS IN THE FIRST 90 DAYS

**Weeks 1–2 — inherit the accounts properly.** For every live client: what was sold, what is actually being delivered, when it is due, who owns each piece, and where the last three deliverables slipped and why. In a small team this usually lives in someone's head and in ClickUp inconsistently. I would rather find the gaps in week one than discover them in a client call.

**Then fix the recurring stuff first.** Reporting, check-ins and status updates are the work that arrives whether or not anyone has capacity. Templated, scheduled, and pre-populated from source data, they stop competing with the work clients actually pay for. This is the single highest-leverage thing to do in a team of your size, and it is where AI belongs first — not in ideation, in the parts nobody wants to do twice.

**Make the AI capability transferable, not personal.** Documented prompt libraries per deliverable type, with a QC gate on every one. The test I hold myself to: a new hire in week two should produce output at the same standard without asking anyone. Undocumented AI skill inside a small agency is a single point of failure wearing the costume of an advantage.

**Put a defensible line under scope.** With a founder-led client relationship, scope creep does not arrive as a request, it arrives as a favour. What I would want agreed early is what triggers a conversation versus what we absorb — because "clients happy" and "team not underwater" only coexist if someone writes that line down.

**Instrument before optimising.** Every account gets one page: what we spend, what it returns, what we are testing, what we killed and why. Not for the client — for us. You cannot tell an over-serviced account from a profitable one without it.

**Ask about the AEO gap out loud.** It is a stated requirement I have not run formally, so in month one I would want to know which clients it matters for and what "working" looks like to them — then build the measurement framework before the tactics, which is the opposite of how most of the market is approaching it.
